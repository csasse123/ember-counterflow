Ember Counterflow

Ambient wind pushes left → right, yet a hot ember can still land upwind and ignite fuel. The fire makes its own wind: the rising plume must entrain air, and that in-draft can reverse the ground flow.

Why against the wind? A hot fire is a chimney. Rising air must be replaced: near the ground, air is drawn toward the flame from all sides. Downwind of the fire, that in-draft can be stronger than the ambient wind → a reverse-flow pocket (cyan). Embers lofted in the plume can fall into that pocket and be carried upwind, landing on unburned fuel. Full wildfire models (e.g. FIRETEC-class) solve this with coupled CFD; here we use a reduced plume + entrainment field so you can feel the mechanism with sliders.
