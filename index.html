Can an ember go against the wind?

Orange / yellow = hot fire and smoke heat. Magenta arrows = air going against the free-stream wind (if the equations produce it). Gold sparks = embers. Watch the side view below — it is easier to read.

SIDE VIEW (cut through fire) — easiest place to see reverse ground flow
What you are watching
  1. Purple wind enters from the left (free-stream).
  2. Orange heat rises as a plume (hot air is lighter).
  3. Rising air must be replaced → ground air is pulled toward the fire.
  4. When that pull fights a strong wind, magenta reverse flow can appear near the ground.
  5. Embers can ride that reverse flow and land on green upwind fuel.
Your intuition — strong wind + strong rising heat makes complex counter-flows — is the right regime. Calm air rarely does this drama.

Can this even be modeled? Yes. Full wildfire codes (e.g. NIST FDS, FIRETEC-class LES) solve 3D Navier–Stokes with buoyancy and track firebrands as particles. Your picture — strong wind + strong rising heat → turbulent counter-flows — is exactly fire–atmosphere coupling. This page runs a coarse but real 3D NS in the browser so you can see the idea; it is not a firefighter forecast tool.
